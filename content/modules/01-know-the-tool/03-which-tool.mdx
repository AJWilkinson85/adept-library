## You need one, maybe two

There is a lot of noise about AI tools. For an owner-operated business, the practical field is small.

**A general assistant** (Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini). This is the one you will use every day: drafting, summarizing, categorizing, thinking through a decision. Pick one, pay for the individual plan (roughly $20 a month at the time of writing), and learn it well. The free tiers are fine for trying things and not fine for relying on. The paid plans give you the better models, longer documents, and the ability to upload files, which matters more than any other feature for the work in this library.

**Whatever your email and accounting already are.** Gmail, Outlook, QuickBooks, Xero. Most of these have added AI features. Some are useful, some are marketing. The rule in this library is that we do the thinking in the general assistant and use the built-in features only when they are clearly better for a specific task. You do not need to switch anything.

**Nothing else, for now.** Not a "custom GPT builder," not an agent platform, not a $99-a-month AI workflow tool. Those are for later, if ever, and usually the general assistant does the job.

<Callout kind="note">
Pricing and plan names change every few months. The principle does not: one paid general assistant, learned deeply, beats five tools used shallowly.
</Callout>

## Claude or ChatGPT?

Both are excellent for the work here. I teach on Claude because it is strong at long documents and careful writing, which is most of what a business needs, and because its handling of uploaded files has been reliable. If your team already uses ChatGPT, stay there; every prompt in this library works in either. What you should not do is split the team across both, because the shared prompt library in Module 2 assumes one tool.

## What "uploading a file" changes

This is the feature that turns a toy into a tool. When you attach your price list, your last ten quotes, or a month of transactions, the model stops guessing about your business and starts working from it. Every module after this one assumes you can attach a file. Test it now.

<Prompt title="Test the file feature">
[Attach any PDF or spreadsheet from your business: a price list, a recent invoice, a menu, a services sheet.]

Read the attached file. In three sentences, tell me what this business sells, who it seems to sell to, and one thing about the pricing that stands out. Then list two questions the document raises that you cannot answer from it alone.
</Prompt>

If the answer is specific to your document, the feature works and you are ready for Module 2. If it is generic, the file did not attach or the plan you are on does not support it.

<Worksheet title="Set up your tool">
- Pick one general assistant and start the paid individual plan.
- Run the file test above with a real document.
- Turn off "improve the model with my data" or its equivalent in the privacy settings. Module 6 covers why; do it now anyway.
- Bookmark the tool in the browser your office actually uses, on the computer where the work happens.
</Worksheet>
