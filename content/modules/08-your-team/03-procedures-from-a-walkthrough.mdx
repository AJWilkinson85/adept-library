## The reason you have no SOPs

Writing a procedure is boring, and the person who knows it best is the busiest person in the building. So it lives in your head, and every new hire learns it by standing next to you, and every time you are out, it gets done slightly wrong. The tool removes the boring part. You talk; it structures.

## Record a walkthrough

Pick one task that goes wrong when you are not there. Open the voice memo app on your phone, or the dictation feature in your notes app, and explain the task the way you would to a new hire standing next to you. Five to ten minutes. Include the parts you would normally skip: what to do when the customer is not home, where the spare key is kept, which vendor to call when the usual one is out. Do not organize it. Just talk.

Then get the transcript (most phones transcribe voice memos now, and any AI tool will transcribe an audio file).

<Prompt title="Procedure from a transcript">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here is a transcript of me explaining how we do [task]: [paste]

Turn this into a written procedure a new hire could follow without me. Sections: when this applies, what you need before you start, the steps in order (numbered, one action per step, short), what to do when it goes wrong (pull every "if this happens" from my transcript into its own list), and who to ask. Keep my wording where it is clear. Where I skipped something a new hire would need, mark it with [GAP] and a question for me. Under one page.
</Prompt>

The [GAP] markers are the point. They are the questions the new hire would have asked at 4pm on a Friday when you were unreachable.

## The review is the training

Hand the draft to the person who does the task second-most. Ask them to do it once following only the paper. Every place they stop and ask is a fix. That review is also the first time they have been trained on the task properly, so the procedure and the training happen in the same afternoon.

<Callout kind="note">
Keep procedures in one place your team can search: a shared folder, a notes app, the same document as the prompt library. A procedure nobody can find is a procedure that does not exist. Date each one; if it has not been touched in a year, it is probably wrong.
</Callout>

## Updating

When something changes, do not rewrite. Paste the current procedure and say what changed: "We switched suppliers; the new one is [name], and the return process is now [describe]." Ask for the updated version and a one-line summary of the change to send to the team. Two minutes.

<Worksheet title="Write the one that hurts">
- Pick the task that goes wrong most often when you are away.
- Record a walkthrough on your phone. Do not stop to organize.
- Run the procedure prompt on the transcript. Answer every [GAP].
- Have someone do the task from the paper alone. Fix what they got stuck on.
- File it where the team can find it, dated. Then pick the next one.
</Worksheet>
