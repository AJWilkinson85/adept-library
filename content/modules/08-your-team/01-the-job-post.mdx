## What is wrong with the usual post

"Seeking a motivated self-starter with excellent communication skills." Every post says it, so it says nothing. The good candidate, the one who is currently employed and only half looking, skims it and moves on. The post that lands with that person describes a real day, a real boss, and a real number.

## Decide three things before you prompt

What does a good Tuesday look like in this job, hour by hour, roughly? What is the one thing the last person in this role got wrong that you will not tolerate again? And what is the pay, actually, as a range you would sign? If you will not put the range in the post, expect half the applicants and the wrong half.

<Prompt title="Job post from a real day">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

The role: [title]. Here is what a typical day looks like: [paste, rough is fine].
The one thing the last person got wrong: [paste].
Pay range: [range]. Hours: [hours]. Location: [where]. Benefits, if any: [list].
Who this job is not for: [one or two honest sentences].

Write a job post under 400 words with these sections in this order: the day (what you will actually do, in the second person), who does well here and who does not, the deal (pay, hours, location, benefits, stated plainly), and how to apply (one specific instruction that takes effort, like "tell us about a time you fixed something nobody asked you to"). No adjectives about the candidate. No "fast-paced" or "wear many hats." My voice.
</Prompt>

The "how to apply" instruction is a filter. The people who follow it are the ones who read the whole post.

<Callout kind="note">
Check the post against your state's rules on what you can and cannot ask or say; pay transparency and non-discrimination language vary. The tool is not a lawyer, and neither is this lesson. When in doubt, ask it to list anything that might be a problem in [your state], then verify with a human who knows.
</Callout>

## Screening the pile

When applications arrive, the tool can sort them against the post the same way it sorts the inbox. Paste the post and the application text, ask for a fit assessment against the four sections, and read the ones it flags as strong first. It will be wrong sometimes. It will also save you an evening.

<Worksheet title="Write the post">
- Write the typical day. Do not polish; bullet points are fine.
- Name the thing you will not tolerate again and put it in the "who does not do well here" section.
- Decide the pay range and put it in the post.
- Run the prompt, edit for voice, and have one employee read it and tell you what is not true.
- Save the recipe. You will hire again.
</Worksheet>
