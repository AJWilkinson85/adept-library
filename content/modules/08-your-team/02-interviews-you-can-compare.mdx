## Why your interviews are hard to trust

You liked the second candidate more. Was it because they were better, or because you were less tired, or because they laughed at your joke? If every interview is a different conversation, you cannot tell. The fix is old and unglamorous: the same questions, in the same order, scored on paper the same afternoon.

## Build the question set once

Six questions is enough for a first interview. Two about the work itself, two about how they handle the things that go wrong in this role, one about working with the people they will work with, one that is just "walk me through your last job, start to finish." The tool is good at turning the job post into those questions, and better at writing the follow-ups you will forget to ask.

<Prompt title="Interview questions and scorecard">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here is the job post: [paste]

Write six interview questions for a first interview, in this order: two about doing the work, two about handling things that go wrong in this role (use the "who does not do well here" section), one about working with the team, one "walk me through your last job." For each question, give two follow-up prompts to use if the answer is vague. Then write a scorecard: for each question, what a weak answer sounds like, what a strong answer sounds like, in one line each, so I can score 1 to 3 right after the interview. Plain text, one page.
</Prompt>

Print it. Score every candidate on paper before you talk to anyone else about them.

## The debrief

After the interview, before your opinion sets, spend five minutes with the tool. Not to decide for you; to separate what you heard from how you felt.

<Prompt title="Interview debrief">
Here is the scorecard with my scores and my rough notes for [candidate first name only]: [paste]

Summarize what this candidate actually said for each question in one line, using only my notes, no inference. Then list any question where my score seems higher or lower than my notes support, and say why. Then list the two things I should verify with a reference. Do not tell me whether to hire them.
</Prompt>

<Callout kind="warn">
First name only, or a letter. No resume, no address, no anything from their application in the tool beyond what is needed to score the answers. A candidate's information is theirs, and this is one of the places where Module 6 is not optional.
</Callout>

## Comparing

When you have scored three candidates on the same six questions, the comparison is a table you can read across. The person with the highest total is not automatically the hire. But the person with the highest total and the one you liked most are usually the same person, and when they are not, that gap is the most useful thing the process tells you.

<Worksheet title="Run one structured interview">
- Generate the questions and scorecard from your job post. Edit the "strong answer" lines until they sound like your standard.
- Use the exact same set for every candidate in this round.
- Score on paper within an hour of each interview.
- Run the debrief prompt on each. Note where it disagrees with your score.
- Check references on the two things it told you to verify.
</Worksheet>
