## The routine that holds the others together

Every recipe in this library produces something: a triage list, an OWED list, a books summary, a set of procedures. The Friday review is where they meet. Twenty minutes, same time every week, one prompt, and you walk into Monday knowing what matters. Owners who do this stop feeling like the business is happening to them.

## What you bring

Four things, all of which you already have if you have done the earlier modules. This week's OWED list from meeting notes. The books summary, or the week's revenue and cash if you only look monthly. Your own notes: what went wrong, what surprised you, what a customer said. And last Friday's "three things for Monday," so you can see whether they happened.

<Prompt title="Friday review">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Last Friday's three things for the week: [paste]. What actually happened with them: [paste, honestly].
This week's OWED list (commitments from meetings): [paste].
This week's numbers: [paste, or say "monthly only, skip"].
My notes from the week, unpolished: [paste].

Write my Friday review in four short sections. DONE AND NOT DONE: last week's three things, each marked done, partly, or not, with my reason in a few words. STILL OWED: from the OWED list, anything past its date or due next week, grouped by person. WHAT THE WEEK SAYS: two or three sentences on what my notes and numbers suggest, in plain language, no advice. THREE THINGS FOR MONDAY: the three most important things for next week, each one sentence, chosen from what is owed and what the week says. Under 250 words.
</Prompt>

## Reading it

"Done and not done" is the accountability section, and it is the one most owners skip. If the same thing has been "partly" for three Fridays, it is either not important or it needs a different approach, and the review will make that obvious in a way that a to-do list never does.

"Three things for Monday" is the output. Write them on paper and put the paper where you will see it Monday morning. Not in an app. On paper.

<Callout kind="note">
The review is for you, not for the team. But if you have a manager or a second in command, running it together, with them bringing their own OWED list, is the best twenty-minute meeting in the business. Make it the only standing meeting you have.
</Callout>

## When to change the recipe

After a month, the sections you never read get cut and the question you keep wishing it asked gets added. Everyone's Friday review looks different after a quarter. That is the recipe working. The quarterly policy-and-recipes check from Module 6 is where you make the change official.

<Worksheet title="First Friday">
- Put twenty minutes on the calendar for this Friday, and every Friday.
- Gather the four inputs. If you do not have an OWED list yet, run this week's meeting notes through the previous lesson's prompt first.
- Run the review. Write the three things on paper.
- Next Friday, bring the paper back and mark it honestly.
- After four Fridays, edit the prompt to fit how you actually think.
</Worksheet>
