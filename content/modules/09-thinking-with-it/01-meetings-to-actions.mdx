## What a meeting is for

A meeting produces decisions and commitments. Everything else is conversation. Most meeting notes capture the conversation and lose the commitments, which is why the same topic comes back next month. The recipe here does one thing: it separates what was decided and who owes what from everything else, and it does it before you forget.

## Capture, then sort

Capture however is easiest. Typed notes during the call. A voice memo of yourself summarizing right after. A transcript from a video meeting tool, if you have one and everyone has agreed to it. The quality of the capture matters less than doing it within ten minutes of the end.

<Prompt title="Meeting notes to actions">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here are my notes or the transcript from [meeting, who was there, date]: [paste]

Produce three lists and nothing else. DECIDED: each decision in one sentence, as a statement, not a topic. OWED: each commitment as "[name] will [action] by [date]", and where no date was said, write "[date?]". OPEN: questions raised but not answered, one line each. If something was discussed at length but nothing was decided or owed, leave it out. Under 200 words total.
</Prompt>

The "[date?]" markers go back to the people who made the commitment, today, with a one-line message: "You said you'd send the revised quote; when should I expect it?" That message is the whole difference between a business where things get done and one where they get discussed.

<Callout kind="warn">
Recording other people requires their agreement, and in some states everyone's. Say it out loud at the start: "I'm going to record this so I get the details right, okay?" For customers and vendors, typed notes are simpler and safer. Module 6 covers what may be pasted; a customer's meeting notes usually may, with names of third parties removed.
</Callout>

## The follow-up message

While you are there, ask for the summary to send. Same three lists, addressed to the people in the room, in your voice, under 120 words. Send it within the hour. People who receive a clear summary of what they committed to tend to do it, because now it is written down and someone noticed.

## Site visits and phone calls count

The recipe works as well on a five-minute phone call with a supplier as a one-hour planning meeting. Get in the habit of a thirty-second voice memo after every call that produced a commitment. "Talked to Ray at the lumber yard, he'll hold the order until Thursday, I owe him a decision on the cedar by Wednesday." That is enough.

<Worksheet title="Run it on your next meeting">
- Capture notes from the next meeting or call where anything is decided.
- Run the prompt within ten minutes of the end.
- Chase every "[date?]" the same day.
- Send the summary to everyone who was there.
- Add the recipe to the library, and start a running "OWED" list that you re-run the prompt against each Friday (next lesson).
</Worksheet>
