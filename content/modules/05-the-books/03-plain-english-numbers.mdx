## The reports exist. Nobody reads them.

Your accounting tool can produce a profit and loss statement, a balance sheet, and an aging report in one click. Most owners do not look at them, not because the numbers are unimportant but because the format answers questions accountants ask, not questions owners ask. "Can I afford to hire?" is not a line on any report. The tool can bridge that gap, as long as you give it the reports and ask the real question.

## The recipe

<Prompt title="Explain my numbers">
Here is my business brief: [paste]
Attached: my profit and loss for [period], the same period last year, and my current balance sheet. [Attach the exports or paste them.]

Explain these to me like a sharp friend who happens to be a CFO, in plain English, no jargon without a definition. Cover:
1. What actually happened this period in three sentences.
2. The three numbers that changed most versus last year, and your best guess at why, based only on what's in the reports and my brief.
3. Cash: how many months of my typical monthly expenses do I have on hand, shown with the arithmetic.
4. Anything in these reports that a careful reader would ask me about.

Then answer this specific question: [your real question, e.g. "Can I afford to add a second technician at $62,000 a year starting in January?"]. Show the assumptions and the math. If you can't answer it from these reports, tell me exactly what else you'd need.
</Prompt>

<Callout kind="warn">
This is a reading aid, not advice. The tool does not know your tax situation, your plans, or what your accountant knows. Use it to understand your numbers well enough to ask your accountant better questions, and recompute any arithmetic that matters before you act on it.
</Callout>

## Questions worth asking every quarter

Once the recipe works, the questions get more interesting. Which service line actually makes money once you count the labor? What does a slow month cost, and how many can you absorb? If you raised prices eight percent, what happens to the year, assuming you lose some percentage of customers? None of these need a new tool. They need the reports, the brief, and a clear question.

## The habit

Quarterly, at minimum. Monthly is better and takes fifteen minutes once the recipe exists. Keep the answers in the "Monthly notes" document from the last lesson. In a year you will have a written record of what you understood about the business and when, which is worth more than it sounds.

<Worksheet title="Ask one real question">
- Export the P&L for the last full quarter and the same quarter last year, plus the current balance sheet.
- Write down the one question about money you have been avoiding.
- Run the recipe with that question. Recompute the cash-months arithmetic yourself.
- Send the tool's "a careful reader would ask" list to your accountant as your questions for the next call.
- Add the recipe to the library with CHECK: "All arithmetic recomputed. Nothing acted on without the accountant seeing it."
</Worksheet>
