## Why owners are bad at collecting

You did the work, you sent the invoice, and 45 days later you are writing an email that is either too soft because you like the customer or too sharp because you are tired of it. Collections is the one place where a fixed sequence, sent on a fixed schedule, beats judgment. The judgment goes into writing the sequence once.

## The sequence

Four messages, each tied to a number of days past due, each a little firmer. The tone shift is the whole design: a friendly nudge, a plain reminder, a clear statement of what happens next, and a final notice that names the date and the consequence. Same voice throughout. No threats you will not follow through on.

<Prompt title="Collections sequence">
Here is my business brief, including our payment terms: [paste]

Write four messages for an unpaid invoice, with placeholders for [name], [invoice number], [amount], and [due date]:
1. Day 3 past due: friendly, assumes it was missed, includes how to pay.
2. Day 14: plain reminder, states the amount and date, asks if there is a problem with the work or the invoice.
3. Day 30: states clearly that the account is past due, what late terms apply from our brief, and that we will pause new work until it is settled.
4. Day 45: final notice, states the date after which we will [what we actually do: send to collections, file in small claims, stop service], and offers one last way to resolve it by phone.
Each under 100 words, my voice, no exclamation points, no "as per." Then tell me anything in the sequence that should be checked against my state's rules on late fees or collections.
</Prompt>

<Callout kind="warn">
Only threaten what you will do. If you have never sent anyone to collections and do not intend to start, say "pause service" at day 45, not "collections." A customer who calls a bluff teaches every other customer the bluff.
</Callout>

## Sending on schedule

The sequence lives in the prompt library with the day numbers. Every Friday, the books routine from Module 5 gives you the aged receivables list. Paste it and ask which invoices are due for which message today. Send them. This is ten minutes a week and it is the difference between 45-day and 25-day average collection, which for many small businesses is the difference between borrowing and not.

## When to stop

Past day 45, the tool is out of the picture. It is a phone call from you, then a decision: payment plan, collections agency, small claims, or write-off. Write the decision and the date on the invoice. The pattern of write-offs, looked at once a year, is also the list of customers to stop taking on, and the quote recipe in Module 4 is where you add "deposit required" for the kind of job that keeps ending up here.

<Worksheet title="Set up collections">
- Check your payment terms in the business brief. If they are not there, write them now: due date, late fee if any, what happens at 45 days.
- Generate the four messages. Check the state-rules note.
- Find every invoice currently past due and send the message that matches its age. Today.
- Add "collections check" to the Friday routine.
- Save the recipe, Tier 1 for messages 1 and 2, Tier 2 for 3 and 4.
</Worksheet>
