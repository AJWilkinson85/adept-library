## The first draft is never the one to send

Every owner has sent the reply written at the peak of the feeling. It was accurate, it was justified, and it cost a customer and sometimes a review. The tool's job here is not to write the reply. It is to take your first draft, the honest one, and hand you back the version that gets what you actually want, which is usually for this to be over and the customer to stay.

## Write the angry version first

Seriously. Type what you want to say, all of it. Then paste it in.

<Prompt title="Complaint reply, second draft">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here is the complaint, with the customer's name replaced by "the customer": [paste]

Here is what actually happened from our side: [paste, honestly, including what we got wrong if anything]

Here is my first draft of a reply, which I am not going to send: [paste]

Write the reply I should send. Keep anything in my draft that is true and useful. Remove anything that argues, explains why they are wrong, or defends. Acknowledge the specific thing they experienced in the first sentence. Say what we will do, with a date if there is one. If we made a mistake, say so in plain words. If we did not, do not pretend we did; just do not argue. End with a way to reach me directly. Under 150 words. Then tell me, in one line, what my first draft was really trying to say, and whether the reply says it.
</Prompt>

That last line is a small mirror. Use it.

<Callout kind="warn">
Never paste the customer's full name, address, phone, or account details. The complaint itself is fine with the name swapped out. If the complaint mentions health, an injury, or anything that could become a legal matter, stop: this is a phone call and possibly an insurer, not a recipe.
</Callout>

## When to call instead

Anything with the words "lawyer," "injured," "unsafe," or "report you" is a call, today, from you, and a note to your insurer if it touches property or a person. The tool can help you prepare for the call (paste the complaint and ask for the three questions to ask and the two things not to say), but it does not make the call.

## Tier 2, always

Complaint replies are read by the owner, every word, before they go. If it is a one-star public review, it is also slept on. The temptation to let a good draft go straight out is strongest exactly when it is most dangerous.

<Worksheet title="Rewrite one you regret">
- Find a complaint reply you sent that you would write differently now. Run it through the prompt.
- Compare. Note what the recipe removed.
- Save the recipe, Tier 2, with the "call instead" words listed in the CHECK line.
- Next real complaint: write the angry draft, run the prompt, wait an hour, send.
</Worksheet>
