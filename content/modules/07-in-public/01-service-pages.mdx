## Why most AI-written websites are worse

Ask a model to "write a homepage for a plumbing company" and you get the same page every plumber gets: "Your trusted partner for all your plumbing needs." Nobody hired anyone off that sentence. The tool defaults to the average of everything it has read, and the average is beige.

The fix is not a cleverer prompt. It is the business brief from Module 2, with the "how we sound" section doing the heavy lifting, plus one thing most owners skip: examples of the way you actually talk to customers.

## Collect the raw material first

Before you write anything, gather three things. Two or three emails or texts you have sent customers that sound like you on a good day. The five questions customers ask before they book (you know them; write them down). And the one thing you do differently from the two competitors you lose jobs to. If you cannot name it, that is the first problem to solve, and no page will fix it.

<Prompt title="Rewrite a service page">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here are three messages I wrote to customers that sound like me: [paste]

Here are the five questions customers ask before they book: [list]

Here is the current text of my [service name] page: [paste, or say "there isn't one"]

Rewrite the page in my voice. Rules: answer the five questions somewhere on the page, in the order a nervous customer would ask them. Use the words from my messages, not marketing words. No "trusted partner," no "needs," no exclamation points. Under 350 words. End with one plain sentence about what happens after they contact us. Then list any claim you made that I should verify before publishing.
</Prompt>

That last line matters. The model will invent a warranty length or a response time if you let it. The list of claims is your check.

<Callout kind="note">
Write one page this way, live with it for a week, then do the rest. The second page is faster because you will edit the brief's "how we sound" section with whatever you fixed on the first one.
</Callout>

## The "about" page

The about page is the one people read when they are deciding between you and someone cheaper. It should be one true story, not a mission statement. Tell the tool the story out loud, badly, and let it shape it.

<Prompt title="About page from a story">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here is the story of how this business started and what I care about, told badly: [paste, two or three paragraphs, do not polish]

Write an "about" page under 250 words. Keep my details and my phrasing wherever it works. First person. No adjectives about ourselves ("passionate," "dedicated"); show it with a specific instead. One sentence at the end about who we are a good fit for, and one about who we are not.
</Prompt>

"Who we are not" is the sentence that gets you better customers. Keep it.

<Worksheet title="One page, your voice">
- Gather the three raw materials: your messages, the five questions, the one difference.
- Run the service page prompt on your most-visited page. Verify every claim on the list it gives you.
- Read it out loud. Anything you would not say to a customer's face gets cut.
- Publish it, and put the prompt in the library as "Service page rewrite" with a CHECK line: "Verify the claims list. No promised dates or prices not on the sheet."
</Worksheet>
