## The only marketing most small businesses need

Past customers forget you exist. Not because they were unhappy; because a year passed. One short email a month, from a person, about something real, is the cheapest work you will ever win. The reason nobody sends it is the blank page. So do not start from a blank page.

## The raw material is your month

At the end of the month you have: jobs you did, a question a customer asked that others probably have, something seasonal coming, maybe a price or service change. That is the email. You are not writing content; you are reporting.

<Prompt title="Monthly update from notes">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here are my notes on this month, unpolished: [paste: two or three jobs worth mentioning without customer names, one question a customer asked, anything seasonal or changing next month]

Write a monthly email to past customers. Under 200 words. Plain text, no images, no headings. Open with the most interesting thing from the notes, not with "hope you're well." One useful thing they can act on (the seasonal item or the question). One sentence at the end about how to book, no urgency language. Subject line under 6 words, specific to the content. In my voice. Then list anything I should double-check.
</Prompt>

## What to leave out

Discounts, unless you would have given them anyway. "Limited time." Anything that reads like it came from a marketing calendar. The whole point is that it sounds like you wrote it on a Tuesday afternoon, because you did, with help.

<Callout kind="note">
Send it from your normal email address, or from a plain sender in whatever tool you use, to people who gave you their email as customers. If you have more than a couple hundred addresses, use a real email tool with an unsubscribe link; the law requires it and it protects your sending reputation.
</Callout>

## The routine

Last Friday of the month, twenty minutes. Five minutes writing notes, one prompt, ten minutes editing, send. Put it on the calendar next to the books routine from Module 5. Two recurring blocks, both under half an hour, and the business sounds alive all year.

<Worksheet title="Send one">
- Write this month's notes. Three jobs, one question, one seasonal thing. Five minutes.
- Run the prompt. Cut anything that does not sound like you.
- Check the list of things to verify.
- Send it to your customer list, or to the last fifty customers if you do not have a list yet.
- Add "Monthly update" to the library and put the Friday block on the calendar.
</Worksheet>
