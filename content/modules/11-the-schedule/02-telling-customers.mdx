## Four messages, written once

Almost every customer complaint about a service business is about time: nobody confirmed, nobody reminded, nobody said they were late. Four short messages, written once in your voice and saved, cover nearly all of it. The tool writes them in a minute; the discipline is sending them.

<Prompt title="The four scheduling messages">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Write four short text messages, each under 50 words, in my voice, with placeholders in square brackets for names, dates, and times:
1. CONFIRMATION, sent when a job is booked: what, when, who is coming, one sentence on what they should do to prepare (if anything).
2. REMINDER, sent the afternoon before: when we will arrive, a window not a time, and a way to reach us if that no longer works.
3. RUNNING LATE, sent as soon as we know: honest, a new window, no excuses longer than four words.
4. RESCHEDULE, sent when we have to move them: apologize once, offer two specific alternatives, make it easy to say neither works.
No exclamation points. No "we apologize for any inconvenience."
</Prompt>

## The window, not the time

Say "between 8 and 10" and arrive at 9:40 and you are on time. Say "9" and arrive at 9:40 and you are late. The confirmation and reminder messages should always use a window. If your scheduling tool sends its own confirmations, check that they do; most default to a time.

## Sending

These are Tier 1 (skim and send) for confirmation and reminder. Running late and reschedule are still Tier 1 but they are the ones to send the moment you know, not when you get a chance. The customer who hears at 8:15 that you will be there at 10:30 instead of 9 is mildly annoyed. The one who finds out at 9:20 by calling you is gone.

<Callout kind="note">
If you use a scheduling app with message templates, paste these four into it and delete the defaults. If you send by hand, keep them in the notes app on your phone. Do not use the tool to send messages; use it to write the messages you send.
</Callout>

## The quiet customer

The one who never confirmed the reminder. Before the crew leaves, one message: "Still good for this morning? Reply Y or call me." A no-show costs you a half day. The message costs nothing.

<Worksheet title="Replace your templates">
- Generate the four messages. Edit until they sound like you on a good day.
- Check every one uses a window, not a time.
- Load them into your scheduling app or your phone.
- For the next ten jobs, send all four when they apply. Count the "where are you" calls. It will be fewer.
- Add the recipe to the library, Tier 1.
</Worksheet>
