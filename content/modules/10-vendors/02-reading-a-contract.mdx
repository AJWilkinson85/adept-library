## What this is and is not

The tool is not a lawyer, and this lesson does not replace one. What it does is turn a 14-page vendor agreement into a one-page list of the clauses that could cost you money or lock you in, so that the ten minutes you get with an actual lawyer are spent on the right paragraphs, or so you know which three things to push back on before signing a small agreement you were never going to pay a lawyer to read.

## The read

Paste the whole agreement. Length does not matter to the tool; it matters to you, which is the point.

<Prompt title="Contract walk-through">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Here is a contract I am considering signing with [vendor] for [what]: [paste the full text]

Walk me through it as a careful business owner would, not as a lawyer. List, with the section number for each: the term and how it renews, how and when either side can end it and what it costs to, what I am committing to pay and whether that can change, what happens if I pay late, what they promise to deliver and what happens if they do not, who owns any data or work product, any limits on their liability, any clause that lets them change the terms, and anything that is unusual for an agreement like this. For each, one line on why it matters to a business my size. Then list the five things I should ask them to change, in order of how much they matter. Say plainly which items a lawyer should look at.
</Prompt>

<Callout kind="warn">
Auto-renewal and termination fees are where small businesses get hurt most. If the walk-through shows a term that renews for a full year unless you cancel 90 days ahead, put the cancellation date on the calendar now, before you sign, whether or not you plan to cancel.
</Callout>

## Pushing back

Most vendor agreements are templates, and most templates have give in them. Ask for the change in writing, one email, each item its own line, phrased as a request rather than a demand. The tool drafts it from the "five things to change" list in under a minute. You will not get all five. You will usually get two, and one of them will be the auto-renewal.

## Keeping track

After you sign, run one more prompt: from the final agreement, list every date and deadline (renewal, notice period, price increase windows, delivery dates) as a list of calendar entries. Put them on the calendar. Contracts do not hurt you on the day you sign them; they hurt you on the day you forgot.

<Worksheet title="Read one before you sign it">
- Take the next agreement that lands, or one you signed last year and never read.
- Run the walk-through. Note the auto-renewal and termination terms first.
- Draft the pushback email for the top three items. Send it.
- After signing, extract the dates and put every one on the calendar.
- If any line in the walk-through says "lawyer," that is the line to spend the money on.
</Worksheet>
