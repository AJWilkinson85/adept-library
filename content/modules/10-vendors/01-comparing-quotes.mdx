## The problem with three quotes

One vendor quotes per unit, one per month, one bundles delivery and the other two do not. One has a 12-month minimum buried in the terms. You end up comparing the headline numbers and picking the one that felt best on the phone. The tool is good at exactly the tedious part: normalizing everything to the same basis and listing what is different.

## Decide the basis first

Before you paste anything, write down how you want to compare: total cost over what period, including what (delivery, setup, support, taxes), for what quantity. "Total cost for 12 months at 200 units a month, delivered, including any setup fees" is a basis. "Which is cheapest" is not.

<Prompt title="Normalize vendor quotes">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

I am comparing quotes for [what you are buying]. Compare them on this basis: [your basis sentence].

Quote A from [vendor]: [paste the quote text or the numbers and terms]
Quote B from [vendor]: [paste]
Quote C from [vendor]: [paste]

Produce a table with one row per vendor and these columns: total cost on my basis, what that total includes, what it excludes, minimum commitment, cancellation terms, lead time, and anything unusual in the terms. Where a quote does not say, write "not stated" rather than guessing. Below the table, list every assumption you had to make to fill it in, and the three questions I should ask each vendor before deciding.
</Prompt>

The "not stated" rule is the whole point. The vendor who left out cancellation terms is the one you call first.

<Callout kind="note">
Vendor quotes are business documents, not customer data, and are usually fine to paste. If a quote contains another customer's pricing or anything marked confidential, strip it. Your own pricing to your customers stays out too; you are comparing what you buy, not what you sell.
</Callout>

## Asking for a better price

Once the table exists, the negotiation email writes itself. Paste the table and ask for a short message to the vendor you prefer, naming the two specific places their quote is worse than a competitor's, and asking whether they can match. Specific beats "can you do better" every time.

<Worksheet title="Compare the next purchase">
- Pick a purchase you have two or more quotes for, or request a second quote on something you buy regularly.
- Write your basis sentence.
- Run the normalize prompt. Read the assumptions list.
- Ask each vendor the three questions before deciding.
- Save the recipe. Add your basis sentence to the business brief under "how we buy."
</Worksheet>
