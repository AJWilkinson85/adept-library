## The one rule

The model never sets a price. It looks prices up in the list you give it, multiplies by the quantities you give it, and shows its work. If something is not on the list, it says so and stops. That rule is not about distrust; it is about where the judgment lives. Pricing is your judgment. Assembling the line items is clerical, and clerical is what the tool is for.

## What you need

A price list in any readable form: a spreadsheet, a PDF, a table pasted as text. Rates per unit, per hour, per visit, packages, minimums, whatever you actually use. If your pricing lives in your head, this module is the excuse to write it down; it will pay for itself the first week.

## The recipe

<Prompt title="Assemble a quote from the scope">
Here is my business brief: [paste]
Attached is my current price list. Use only these prices.

Below is a scope of work. Build a line-item quote from it:
- One line per included item, with quantity, unit, rate from the price list, and line total.
- Show the arithmetic for each line and the subtotal.
- Apply only the minimums, fees, or taxes that my brief or price list specify, each as its own line.
- If any scope item has no matching price on the list, do not guess. Put it in a section called "Needs a price from Aaron" with your best description of what it is.
- Do not add items that are not in the scope.

Then give me the quote as a clean table I can paste into my quoting tool.

Scope:
[paste the scope from the last lesson]
</Prompt>

<Callout kind="warn">
Recompute the totals. The model's arithmetic is usually right and occasionally off by a line, and a quote is the worst place to find out. Paste the table into a spreadsheet and let the spreadsheet add it up. That is the CHECK line, and it is not optional.
</Callout>

## When the list does not cover it

"Needs a price from Aaron" is the point of the recipe. It hands you exactly the items that require judgment and nothing else. You price those, add them, and the quote is done. Over time, items that keep showing up there get added to the price list, and the section gets shorter.

## Options and packages

If you like to offer a good, better, best version, ask for it explicitly: "Build three versions: the scope as written, a version that adds [upgrade], and a version that removes [optional item]. Same rules for each." The model handles the bookkeeping; you decide which three to offer.

<Worksheet title="Price the job you scoped">
- Get your price list into a file the tool can read. If it does not exist, write the twenty most common items today.
- Run the quote recipe on the scope from lesson 1.
- Paste the table into a spreadsheet and recompute every total.
- Price whatever landed in "Needs a price from Aaron." Ask yourself whether it should be on the list.
- Save the recipe with CHECK: "Totals recomputed in a spreadsheet. No line not on the price list."
</Worksheet>
