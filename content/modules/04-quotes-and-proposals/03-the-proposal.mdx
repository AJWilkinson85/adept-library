## Most proposals are too long

The customer wants three things: what you will do, what it costs, and why you rather than the other quote. A page and a half, at most. Everything else is you reassuring yourself. The recipe below produces the short version and is stubborn about it.

## The recipe

<Prompt title="Write the proposal">
Here is my business brief: [paste]
Examples of proposals I've sent that I liked: [paste one or two, or "none, use my brief's voice"]

Using the scope and quote below, write a proposal with these sections and nothing else:
1. A two-sentence opening that names their situation specifically (from the scope's summary), not a generic greeting.
2. What we'll do: the included items, in plain language, grouped sensibly. Not a copy of the line items.
3. What it costs: the total, and the line-item table exactly as given.
4. What's not included and what we're assuming: from the scope, in a short list.
5. Why us: three short sentences drawn only from my brief. No superlatives.
6. Next step: one specific action and how long the price is good for, per my brief.

Under 450 words excluding the table. My voice. No exclamation points. Do not add guarantees, timelines, or terms that aren't in my brief.

Scope: [paste]
Quote: [paste]
</Prompt>

## When you know you are competing

Add one line: "The customer is getting two other quotes. In section 5, address the one thing a cheaper competitor usually skips in our kind of work, per my brief, without naming anyone." That is the only place the proposal gets persuasive, and it does so with a fact, not adjectives.

<Callout kind="note">
If the recipe keeps producing something you would not send, the fix is almost always in the brief: the "how we sound" section is thin, or "why us" has nothing concrete in it. Improve the brief and every proposal improves.
</Callout>

## Formatting

Paste the output into whatever you already use: your quoting tool, a document template with your logo, or the body of an email. The model should not be producing the final document; it produces the words. Keeping the design in your existing template is faster and looks more like you.

## The whole chain

Notes to scope, scope to quote, quote to proposal. Three recipes, three checks. For a straightforward job, that is under fifteen minutes from hanging up the phone to a proposal in the customer's inbox, and the scope is more complete than the one you used to write by hand at ten at night.
