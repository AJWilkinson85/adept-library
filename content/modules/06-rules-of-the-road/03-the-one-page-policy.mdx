## Why one page

A policy nobody reads is not a policy. A page pinned at the top of the prompt library, read once by every new person, is. It should say what tool you use, what may and may not go in, which tasks get which level of review, and where the recipes live. That is it. You have written every piece of it in the last five modules; this lesson assembles them.

## The five sections

**1. The tool.** Which assistant the business uses and who has an account. "We use [tool] on the paid plan. Ask [name] for access. Do not use other AI tools for business work."

**2. What never goes in.** The paragraph from lesson 1 of this module.

**3. The two tiers.** The paragraph from lesson 2.

**4. The recipes.** "Every recurring task has a recipe in the prompt library. Use the recipe. If it produces something bad, fix the recipe, not the draft, and tell [name]."

**5. The check.** "Nothing the tool writes leaves the building without a person reading it against the recipe's CHECK line. If you don't know what the check is, ask."

## When the tools change

They will, roughly quarterly. New models, new features, new pricing. Most of it does not matter to you. The rule: revisit the policy and the recipes once a quarter, in a fifteen-minute block, and ask three questions. Did the tool we use change in a way that affects the data rule? Is there a recipe that has been producing worse results? Is there a task we are still doing by hand that now clearly belongs in Tier 1? Change what needs changing, leave the rest.

## What you have now

A business brief that makes the tool useful. A prompt library with recipes for the inbox, quotes, and the books, each with its check. A data rule and a two-tier rule. A one-page policy. And, if you did the worksheets, a Friday follow-up routine and a monthly books routine that used to not exist.

That is what the working session produces in four hours. You did it in a few evenings, on your own material, which is the only way it sticks.
