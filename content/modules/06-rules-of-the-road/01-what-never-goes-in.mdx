## The line, and why it matters

Everything you paste into an AI tool leaves your building. Where it goes depends on the tool, the plan, and the settings, and those change. So the working rule is simple: assume anything you paste could be seen by someone at the tool company, and decide what you are comfortable with on that basis. For most business work, the answer is "most of it, with a few firm exceptions."

## Never

These do not go in, on any plan, in any tool, regardless of settings:

**Payment details.** Card numbers, bank account and routing numbers, anything from a payment form.
**Government identifiers.** Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, passport numbers, EINs of other people's businesses.
**Passwords and keys.** Yours, your customers', your software's.
**Health information about identifiable people.** If you are in a field where this comes up, you likely have legal obligations that the tool's terms do not satisfy.
**Anything you signed an agreement not to share.** Client contracts with confidentiality clauses, information from a business you consult for, anything under NDA.

## Fine

Your own business information: prices, processes, your writing, your policies. Public information. Your own accounting, with account numbers removed. Customer messages, with the exceptions above stripped, provided you are comfortable that a support engineer at the tool company could in principle see them. Most businesses are, and most already send the same content through email, which offers no better guarantee.

## The gray zone

Customer names and addresses. Employee pay. The specifics of a dispute. Here the answer is "it depends on you," and the useful move is to decide once, write it down, and stop deciding every time.

<Prompt title="Draft our data rule">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Help me write a one-paragraph rule for what my team may and may not paste into AI tools. Start from these fixed rules: never payment details, government IDs, passwords, health information, or anything under NDA. Then ask me three questions to settle the gray zone for my business (customer contact details, employee information, dispute specifics), and write the final paragraph in plain language a new hire would understand. Under 150 words.
</Prompt>

<Callout kind="note">
Also turn off "use my conversations to improve the model" (or its equivalent) in every account your business uses. It does not change the "never" list, but it narrows where your data goes, and it takes thirty seconds.
</Callout>

## The practical habit

Before pasting a customer message or a bank export, look for the five "never" items and delete them. It becomes automatic in a week. For bank exports, delete the account number column. For emails, delete signatures with phone numbers if that is your line. The recipes in this library already include these steps in their CHECK lines; this lesson is why they are there.

<Worksheet title="Set the line">
- Run the data-rule prompt and produce your paragraph.
- Turn off model-training data sharing in every AI account the business uses.
- Go back to the triage and categorization recipes and make sure their privacy steps match your paragraph.
- Put the paragraph at the top of the prompt library document.
</Worksheet>
