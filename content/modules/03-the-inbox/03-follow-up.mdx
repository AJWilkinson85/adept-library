## Where the money actually leaks

Ask an owner what they lose to bad follow-up and they will guess low. Quotes that were never chased. Invoices that slipped past thirty days because nobody wanted to send the awkward email. Customers who said "let me think about it" in March and never heard from you again. None of this is a skill problem. It is a remembering problem, and remembering is exactly what a weekly routine fixes.

## The list

The routine needs one input: a short list of open items. Most businesses can export this from their quoting or invoicing tool in a minute (open estimates, invoices past due). If not, a spreadsheet with four columns is enough: name, what it is, amount, date sent.

## The recipe

<Prompt title="Weekly follow-up batch">
Here is my business brief: [paste]

Below is a list of open items: quotes that haven't been answered and invoices that are past due. For each one, draft a short follow-up in my voice. Rules:
- Quotes under 14 days old: friendly, one question, offer to answer anything. Under 60 words.
- Quotes over 14 days: ask directly whether they'd like to proceed or whether we should close the file. Under 60 words.
- Invoices 1 to 30 days past due: assume they forgot. Warm. Include the amount and a payment link placeholder. Under 70 words.
- Invoices over 30 days: polite, firm, name the date, say what happens next per my brief. Under 80 words.
Never offer a discount. Never apologize for following up. Address each by first name.

Output one draft per item, labeled with the name and amount, in the order I should send them.

The list:
[paste]
</Prompt>

You read down the list, delete the two you have a reason not to send, paste the rest. Ten minutes, every Friday.

<Callout kind="note">
The tone rules above are one business's rules. Yours might be gentler on invoices or firmer on quotes. Edit the rules once, in the recipe, and every Friday inherits the change.
</Callout>

## The one thing to check

Amounts and dates. The tool copies them from your list, and if your list is wrong the email is wrong. That is the CHECK line for this recipe, and it is the whole reason the list should come from an export rather than from memory.

## What this is worth

Do the math once. Take last quarter's quotes that went unanswered, multiply by your close rate on quotes you did chase, and you have the number. For most owner-operated businesses it is the most valuable ten minutes in this library.

<Worksheet title="Run your first Friday">
- Export or build the open-items list for this week.
- Run the batch recipe. Adjust the tone rules until the drafts sound like you.
- Send them. Note how many replies you get in the following week.
- Put a recurring fifteen-minute block on Friday's calendar called "Follow-up batch" and add the recipe to the prompt library.
</Worksheet>
